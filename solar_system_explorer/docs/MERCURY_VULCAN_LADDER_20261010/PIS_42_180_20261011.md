No mechanism offered; these are the two angles in the table I would keep looking at, because a coincidence of *lines* is rarer than a coincidence of sums.

**Heliocentric reading.** If the 5–7 Pis are counted about Sol per Earth year, they are not a moon at all: 5 Pis is a 229.5-day orbit at 0.734 AU (Venus is 5.11 Pis at 0.723 AU); 7 Pis is 163.9 d at 0.586 AU, between Venus and Mercury. Le Verrier's Vulcan (19.7 d, 0.143 AU) is 58 Pis.

**Verdict.** In every frame where a Mercury moon is physical, 5 and 7 Pis fall *outside* the survival annulus: at or beyond the Hill radius in five of the six cases, and in the sixth (7 Pis per Mercury spin) between the annulus ceiling and the Hill radius, where the v4 integrator's particles escaped. Because Pis count orbits, a *small* Pis is a *wide* orbit; the annulus lives at 24–370 Pis per Mercury year (101–1535 per Earth year), and the Hill radius itself is 7.7 Pis per Mercury year. So 5–7 cannot be where we *find* it. It can be how long we *look*: 5 Pis of Earth years is 5/π = 1.59 yr → 2028-05-14, inside BepiColombo's nominal science year and three months after the 2028-02-23 occultation; 7 Pis is 2.23 yr → 2029-01-01, the end of 2028. If 'find Vulcan by 5 Pis, maybe 7' is a clock, it is the right clock.
