# Helioseismology — reading list for review — 2026-10-11

DOI links where they exist (stable); arXiv where the paper is free. Living Reviews in Solar
Physics is fully open access. Grouped so the inference chain can be audited step by step:
what was observed → how it was turned into an interior → where it was disputed → what checked it
from outside. Lunar seismology at the end for the comparison.

Two entries are marked *unverified* — I could not confirm the identifier today; the rest were
checked or are standard.

## 1. The observation (no model)

- Leighton, Noyes & Simon 1962, *Velocity fields in the solar atmosphere I*, ApJ 135, 474 —
  discovery of the 5-minute oscillation. https://doi.org/10.1086/147285
- Deubner 1975, *Observations of low wavenumber nonradial eigenmodes of the Sun*, A&A 44, 371 —
  first k–ω diagram showing the ridges. https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1975A%26A....44..371D
- Duvall 1982, *A dispersion law for solar oscillations*, Nature 300, 242 — the "Duvall law",
  frequencies collapse onto one curve, so they are global modes. https://doi.org/10.1038/300242a0
- Kosovichev et al. 1997, *Structure and rotation of the solar interior: initial results from the
  MDI medium-l program*, Solar Physics 170, 43. https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1004949311268

## 2. The theory that says they are sound

- Ulrich 1970, *The five-minute oscillations on the solar surface*, ApJ 162, 993 — trapped
  acoustic waves predicted. https://doi.org/10.1086/150731
- Leibacher & Stein 1971, *A new description of the solar five-minute oscillation*, ApL 7, 191.
  https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1971ApL.....7..191L

## 3. The inversion — frequencies to c(r) (model enters here)

- Christensen-Dalsgaard 2002, *Helioseismology*, Rev. Mod. Phys. 74, 1073 — the standard review;
  the inversion method and its assumptions are laid out. https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0207403
- Gough et al. 1996, *The seismic structure of the Sun*, Science 272, 1296 — MDI's first
  interior sound-speed profile. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.272.5266.1296
- Basu 2016, *Global seismology of the Sun*, Living Rev. Sol. Phys. 13:2 — open access, current.
  https://doi.org/10.1007/s41116-016-0003-4
- Christensen-Dalsgaard 2021, *Solar structure and evolution*, Living Rev. Sol. Phys. 18:2 —
  open access; §5–6 are the honest accounting of what the models get wrong.
  https://doi.org/10.1007/s41116-020-00028-3
- Kjeldsen, Bedding & Christensen-Dalsgaard 2008, *Correcting stellar oscillation frequencies for
  near-surface effects*, ApJ 683, L175 — the "surface term" fudge, named as such.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.1769

## 4. Rotation and the tachocline

- Thompson, Christensen-Dalsgaard, Miesch & Toomre 2003, *The internal rotation of the Sun*,
  ARA&A 41, 599. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.astro.41.011802.094848
- Schou et al. 1998, *Helioseismic studies of differential rotation in the solar envelope by the
  SOI/MDI*, ApJ 505, 390 — GONG and MDI agree on the shear layer. https://doi.org/10.1086/306146

## 5. Where it is caught out — the abundance problem

- Basu & Antia 2008, *Helioseismology and solar abundances*, Physics Reports 457, 217 — the 1 %
  disagreement below the convection zone after the 2005–2009 abundance revision.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.4590
- Vinyoles et al. 2017, *A new generation of standard solar models*, ApJ 835, 202 — the problem
  restated with current inputs; still open. https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09867

## 6. The core dispute — g-modes

- Fossat et al. 2017, *Asymptotic g modes: evidence for a rapid rotation of the solar core*, A&A
  604, A40 — the claim. https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.00259
- Schunker et al. 2018, *Fragile detection of solar g-modes by Fossat et al.*, Solar Physics
  293:95 — not reproduced. https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.04407
- Scherrer & Gough 2019, *A critical evaluation of recent claims concerning solar rotation*, ApJ
  877, 42 — not reproduced across GOLF, MDI, HMI, LOI, GONG, BiSON.
  https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ab13ad · https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02820

## 7. Local helioseismology and out-of-sample tests

- Gizon & Birch 2005, *Local helioseismology*, Living Rev. Sol. Phys. 2:6 — open access.
  https://doi.org/10.12942/lrsp-2005-6
- Lindsey & Braun 2000, *Seismic images of the far side of the Sun*, Science 287, 1799 — active
  regions predicted on the hidden hemisphere. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.287.5459.1799
- Liewer et al. 2017, *Comparison of far-side helioseismic imaging with STEREO/EUVI*, Solar
  Physics 292 — *unverified identifier*; the paper exists, the volume/page should be checked.

## 8. The independent check — neutrinos

- Borexino Collaboration 2018, *Comprehensive measurement of pp-chain solar neutrinos*, Nature
  562, 505. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0624-y
- Borexino Collaboration 2020, *Experimental evidence of neutrinos produced in the CNO fusion
  cycle in the Sun*, Nature 587, 577. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2934-0
- SNO Collaboration 2002, *Direct evidence for neutrino flavor transformation*, PRL 89, 011301 —
  the solar model was right, the particle physics changed. https://arxiv.org/abs/nucl-ex/0204008

## 9. Lunar seismology — for the comparison

- Latham et al. 1970, *Passive seismic experiment*, Science 167, 455 — Apollo 11; "high Q and
  intense scattering, mutually exclusive on Earth". https://doi.org/10.1126/science.167.3918.455
- Latham et al. 1970, *Seismic data from man-made impacts on the Moon*, Science 170, 620 — the
  Apollo 12 LM impact; low absorption + scattering explains the hour-long reverberation.
  https://doi.org/10.1126/science.170.3958.620
- Nakamura, Latham & Dorman 1982, *Apollo lunar seismic experiment — final summary*, JGR 87,
  A117. https://doi.org/10.1029/JB087iS01p0A117
- Weber et al. 2011, *Seismic detection of the lunar core*, Science 331, 309 — ~330 km core,
  partially molten layer above it. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1199375
- Garcia et al. 2019, *Lunar seismology: an update on interior structure models*, Space Sci. Rev.
  215:50 — open access review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11214-019-0613-y
- Williams et al. 2014, *Lunar interior properties from the GRAIL mission*, JGR Planets 119,
  1546 — moment of inertia I/MR² = 0.3931 ± 0.0002. https://doi.org/10.1002/2013JE004559
- Wieczorek et al. 2013, *The crust of the Moon as seen by GRAIL*, Science 339, 671 — crustal
  porosity ~12 %. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1231530
- Pike 1974, *Depth/diameter relations of fresh lunar craters*, GRL 1, 291 — the depth
  saturation (simple→complex transition). https://doi.org/10.1029/GL001i007p00291
- Pike 1980, *Control of crater morphology by gravity and target type: Mars, Earth, Moon*, Proc.
  LPSC 11, 2159 — the transition diameter scales as 1/g across bodies. *Unverified page range.*
  https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1980LPSC...11.2159P
