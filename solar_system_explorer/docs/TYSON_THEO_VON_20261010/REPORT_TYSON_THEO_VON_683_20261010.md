## 3. The segment that matters here: molecules and shapes (00:44–00:48)

Theo: can you smell in space? Tyson:

> "The molecules are very sparse. It's the world's best vacuum. When you smell, you are inhaling molecules that connect to your olfactory glands … If you taste something, it's a molecule that attaches to your taste buds. Same phenomenon."

Then the shape:

> "Some molecules twist this way, and other molecules twist the other way. The molecule that gives you the flavor of mint … it's exactly the same molecule except in space it twists the opposite way from the molecule that gives you the flavor of caraway … if you write down the chemical equation, it's identical. But if you build them, they turn in opposite directions. So that tells you it's not just the chemical, it's how it's configured so that it attaches to your taste buds."

He does not name the molecule. It is carvone, C₁₀H₁₄O. (R)-(−)-carvone is spearmint; (S)-(+)-carvone is caraway and dill. Same atoms, same bonds, mirror images, and the receptor tells them apart. This is the standard classroom example of enantiomers and it is correct as he gave it.

Then Olestra (captioned "Ostra"): a sucrose polyester fat too large for lipase to cut, so it is tasted and not digested; the 1996 FDA-approved label "may cause abdominal cramping and loose stools"; the WOW chips. His point:

> "We are a sack of chemicals that either do or do not interact with what we put in our mouth. Or what we smell. Or what we taste."

**Why this lands in this repo.** Part One of the fable has Feynman pick up a left glove: no rotation in three dimensions makes it a right glove; the mirror is a rotation we do not have access to. Kant's incongruent counterparts get three points for the same observation. Tyson's tongue is the glove test with a receptor instead of an eye. You do not have to see the handedness. The lock reports it. Mint or caraway is the receipt. That is Feynman's column: a thing that could be wrong, and a measurement that settles it.

The "world's best vacuum" line also sits next to Imhotep's claim in §11 of the fable, that the best medium for an elemental wave has nearly nothing in it, and that sound is the air's complaint. Tyson is saying the complementary thing from the other side: smell is the molecule's arrival, and in a near-vacuum there is nothing to arrive. The two statements are about different carriers. They agree that the medium decides what is kept.

Smaller receipts from the same stretch: Pb is *plumbum*, hence plumber (01:33, correct). Helium was identified in the Sun's spectrum in 1868 before it was isolated on Earth in 1895 (01:34, correct). Theo: "She didn't discover mercury." Tyson: "No, mercury's been around forever" (01:34). Mercury the metal was known in antiquity.
