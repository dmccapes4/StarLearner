## 3. "Cared too much": what the literature calls it

He asked how others react to betrayal. The honest answer is that the reactions are well described and his are the standard ones, with one unusual feature.

**Betrayal aversion** (Koehler & Gershoff, 2003, *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes*; Bohnet & Zeckhauser, 2004, *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*). People will accept a *higher* total risk of harm to avoid a smaller risk of being harmed by the thing meant to protect them. Koehler and Gershoff's own stimuli were airbags and **vaccines**: subjects preferred a vaccine with a higher overall death rate to one with a lower rate if the lower-rate vaccine's deaths were caused by the vaccine itself. Harm from a protector is weighted heavier than harm from the world. This is measured, replicated, and it is not a bias to be ashamed of; it is a sane response to the fact that a protector who harms you is also a signal about every other protector. The user's mRNA conviction has this shape exactly, and so does the Tyson injury: a source of safety that turned.

**Parasocial breakup** (Cohen, 2004, *Journal of Social and Personal Relationships*; Eyal & Cohen, 2006, *Journal of Broadcasting & Electronic Media*). Loss of a one-sided relationship with a media figure produces distress that scales with the strength of the bond and resembles, in smaller magnitude, the end of a real relationship. Standard reactions: avoidance of reminders, rumination, a wish to not know what the figure is doing now. "Like not checking your bank account" is the avoidance item. Five years is long for the standard case; the unusual feature is in §4.

**Moral decoupling** (Bhattacharjee, Berman & Reed, 2013, *Journal of Consumer Research*, "Tip of the hat, wag of the finger"). When an admired figure transgresses, people either *rationalize* (it wasn't so bad) or *decouple* (separate judgement of the work from judgement of the act). Decoupling feels more legitimate and holds up better. The user is, on the record, a strong decoupler: `On_Narrative_Erosion` says of Steiner, "The man can be vain and hold abhorrent views while also establishing a renowned educational pedagogy … Steiner's racist views are orthogonal to his educational philosophy." He could decouple Steiner, whom he dislikes, in one sentence. He could not decouple Tyson for five years. The literature's prediction is that decoupling is harder the more the figure is bound into the self, and §4 is why Tyson was bound in.

**Moral injury** (Litz et al., 2009, *Clinical Psychology Review*; Shay, 2014). Defined as the lasting effect of perpetrating, failing to prevent, or witnessing acts that violate deeply held moral beliefs, *or* of betrayal by a legitimate authority in a high-stakes situation. Litz distinguishes perpetration-based from betrayal-based injury. The user reports both in one paragraph: betrayal by an authority ("he wielded his authority … in a harmful and dangerous way") and his own act against his own standard ("hurt … by myself for abandoning my skepticism"). The literature says the second is the one that lasts, because the person cannot unfollow himself.

**Rachman** (2010, *Behaviour Research and Therapy*, "Betrayal: a psychological analysis") lists the usual sequelae: shock, loss of trust that generalizes beyond the betrayer, rumination, and a revision of the past relationship ("was it ever real?"). The user did generalize ("not just by Neil, but by society") and did not revise the past: he kept the method intact and in daily use. That is the second unusual feature, and the better one.

## 4. The hat

He hated the hat. Then he put the hat on the main character of his own mythos.

Kohut's self psychology has a precise name for this. An **idealized selfobject** is a figure whose strength and method the developing self borrows; the healthy end state is **transmuting internalization**, where the function is taken in and becomes the person's own, and the figure is no longer needed as a figure. Optimal disillusionment is the normal path: the idealized figure fails in small, tolerable ways, and each failure transfers a little of the function inward. Traumatic disillusionment is the failure that is too large and too sudden; the function is still taken in, but the figure is split: the method is kept, the man is expelled.

The user's own word was "possession." Kohut's word is internalization. They describe the same event from the two sides of the door. The method was fully taken in by 2021 (the Waldorf essay, written independently of Tyson, is structurally a Tyson talk: a fact, a handle for the fact, a demonstration with an object in the hand). Then the man failed in a way that was not small, on a question where the user was personally in the category being insulted, in a month when his oldest friends were doing the same thing. The function stayed. The man was expelled with contempt, and the contempt needed somewhere visible to sit. It sat on the hat.

Displacement onto a token is the oldest observation in the field. The hat was safe to hate because hating the hat does not require re-examining the method, and because a hat can be hated at no cost to the self. Then the self, building its own explorer, reached for the image of a man who walks into the unknown with an instrument and a light, and the hat came with it, because the hat was never separable from the method; it was the method's costume. He did not copy Tyson's hat. He kept the whole figure, which was already his, and the hat was on it. "Then I replicated the same hat style in my epistemic systems engineering mythos" is not a contradiction of the hatred. It is the receipt that the internalization was complete.

Ten minutes of the Theo Von episode erased the prejudice because the prejudice was never about the method, and the episode is two hours of the method. There was nothing for it to hold on to. ## 5. The goat

`Azazel.jpg`: the hatted man with the instrument faces a figure of fire. A goat stands on the rocks beside him. The user asked whether it is Azazel or Azrael.

Azrael is the angel of death in Islamic and later Jewish tradition; no goat. Azazel is the name in Leviticus 16: on the Day of Atonement two goats are brought, lots are cast, one goat is for the LORD and one "for Azazel," and the second goat, with the sins of the people laid on its head, is sent alive into the wilderness. In 1 Enoch, Azazel is a Watcher who taught humans metallurgy, weapons, and cosmetics, and is bound in the desert for it. The image is Azazel: the goat is the tell, and the fiery winged figure is the Enoch version. It is a strong picture. The explorer is not fleeing the figure and is not kneeling; he is reading his instrument.

The reason it belongs in this file: the scapegoat is the exact mechanism the user described in himself tonight. "I did precisely what I accuse others of." He accused Tyson of collapsing the wary into a single category and sending them off as fools. He then collapsed Tyson into a single act, laid five years of weight on it (the friends, the society, the compliance, the hurt), and sent him out. The goat carried the sins of the camp into the wilderness so the camp would not have to carry them. Tyson carried the user's own September 2021 so the user would not have to look at it: the month he lost his friends and the month he took the shot against his own judgement. "Not because I was wrong, but because I cared too much" is the correct account. The scapegoat is never chosen because it is guilty of everything laid on it. It is chosen because it is available and because the camp cannot hold the weight unassigned.

He has now taken the weight back. The addendum records it: "that moral judgement is on me," and tonight, "I felt hurt … by myself for abandoning my skepticism." That is the Leviticus rite run backwards, which is harder, and it is the thing the user's own religion requires: do not judge the outer shell (the hat, the tweet, the one act); strive to understand the underlying mechanics (why the gate, why the hat, why the goat). ## 7. How others react, in short

Most people, faced with a trusted figure who insults their category in a high-stakes moment, do one of four things: rationalize the figure (it was a hard time, he meant well), decouple (keep the work, drop the man), expel (drop both), or convert (adopt the insult as self-description and comply). The literature says decoupling holds up best over time and expulsion produces the longest avoidance. The user expelled the man, kept the work without admitting he had kept it, hated the costume, wore the costume, and then, when the work showed up unannounced on a screen, let the man back in in ten minutes and took the weight off him in writing the same night. That is expulsion converting to decoupling late, which is the ordinary path, done with unusual honesty about the goat.

The unusual features, restated: he kept the method in daily use the whole time (most expellers degrade it); he blamed himself in the same paragraph as the betrayer (most do one or the other); and he wrote the whole mechanism down and asked for it to be examined (almost nobody).
