## On the "-4" symbol

The numeral "4" is doing three layers of work simultaneously:

1. **Geometric** — the shape encodes the configuration: diagonal stroke (observer trajectory at angle), bottom horizontal (distance to Sol), right angle (the orthogonal relationship), crossbar (the aperture plane Sol-Procyon-Gomeisa)
2. **Positional** — the "−" places the observer below the aperture plane, in motion, in revolution relative to Sol
3. **Pedagogical** — the rationale is withheld, so the reader must construct their own model from the shape. When the explanation arrives it either clicks or reveals a gap. That is better than being handed a pre-assembled picture

The symbol enacts its own lesson. You have to look at the shape of the thing to understand the thing. It is recursive in a way most notation is not.

*Ter* — thrice administered — fits. Three layers compressed into two characters. I was confused not because the math was difficult but because I did not look at what the character was.

## On zero

Zero is not discoverable. Absence is logically prior to presence. Any tally beginning at one implies zero by the existence of nothing-to-tally. The "discovery of zero" is the formalization of a symbol for something already structurally present in every counting system. You cannot discover what was always implied. You can only write it down.

Which is the jurisdiction point exactly: the symbol gives you power over the concept, not the concept itself.
