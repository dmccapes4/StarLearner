## Overview

Pluto and Charon form the only **true binary system** in the solar system. Their mutual center of mass (barycenter) lies **outside Pluto's surface** — both bodies orbit the barycenter together. This is the star/body ratio inversion: at this distance from Sol, two bodies of comparable mass interact as near-equals.

```
Pluto diameter:   2,376 km
Charon diameter:  1,212 km  (51% of Pluto's diameter)
Charon/Pluto mass ratio:  ~1/8.6  (Charon = 11.6% of Pluto's mass)
Barycenter location: 17,536 km from Pluto's center (outside Pluto's surface radius 1,188 km)
```

## Pluto Orbital Parameters

```
Semi-major axis:        39.482 AU
Perihelion distance:    29.658 AU (inside Neptune's orbit at some epochs)
Aphelion distance:      49.305 AU
Orbital period:         247.94 years
Mean orbital speed:     4.67 km/s
Orbital eccentricity:   0.2488  (high — nearly comet-like)
Inclination to Zecliptic: 17.14°  (largest of any classical solar body)
Argument of perihelion: 113.8°
Longitude of asc. node: 110.3°
```

**Pluto's 17.14° Zecliptic angle is the primary phase displacement indicator.** For comparison, Neptune (1.77°) and Uranus (0.77°) are nearly on the Zecliptic. Pluto is the most phase-displaced classical solar body.

At perihelion (nearest to Sol), Pluto is closer to Sol than Neptune — and at maximum Zecliptic deviation. The phase displacement IS the eccentricity: Pluto's high inclination and high eccentricity are the same phenomenon expressed in two dimensions (angle and distance).

## Charon Orbital Parameters (around Pluto)

```
Semi-major axis (from Pluto):  19,571 km
Orbital period:                6.3872 days  (=Pluto's rotation period — tidally locked)
Orbital eccentricity:          0.0022  (nearly circular)
Inclination to Pluto's equator: 0.0°  (equatorial orbit)
Inclination to Zecliptic:      ~96-122° (oscillates with Pluto's axial wobble)
```

**Pluto and Charon are doubly tidally locked:**
- Pluto always shows the same face to Charon
- Charon always shows the same face to Pluto
- The system rotates as a rigid body about the external barycenter
- Orbital period = rotation period of both bodies = 6.387 days

**The mutual tidal lock is the binary echo property.** Two bodies of comparable mass in mutual synchronous orbit. No other planet/moon pair in the solar system does this.

## Pluto Surface

```
Surface composition:    Nitrogen ice (N₂), methane ice (CH₄), carbon monoxide ice (CO)
Atmospheric pressure:   ~1 Pa at surface  (millionths of Earth's pressure)
Surface temperature:    ~40 K (−233°C)
Tombaugh Regio:         Large heart-shaped nitrogen ice plain (positive Sputnik Planitia)
Surface structure:      Ice mountains (H₂O ice, up to 3,500 m), nitrogen glaciers,
                        possible subsurface liquid water ocean
```

**Why Pluto has mostly nitrogen ice on its surface:**  
At 40 K, nitrogen condenses and stays. Water ice at 40 K is a rigid rock — it FORMS the mountains. Pluto's "rock" is water ice. Nitrogen ice is the low-temperature volatile that coats the surface and flows as glaciers.

## Charon Surface — The Phase Displacement Made Visible

```
Surface composition:    Water ice (H₂O), ammonia ice (NH₃ hydrates)
                        NO nitrogen ice  (completely different from Pluto)
Surface color:          Gray (darker than Pluto)
Makin Mons:             Large reddish polar cap (Mordor Macula) — reddish organic materials
Temperature:            ~53 K  (slightly warmer than Pluto at poles due to reddish absorption)
Internal structure:     Rocky core, thin ice mantle
```

**The key observation — Charon has water ice, Pluto does not (primarily):**

Pluto's interior: estimated 70% rock, 30% water ice (subsurface).  
Charon's surface: dominated by **water ice** — the very thing that is buried inside Pluto.

**The mechanism (user's insight):**  
The dense gas-phase Pluto (thick nitrogen atmosphere cycling volatile ices) effectively **squeezes the water to the surface of Charon**. This is phase displacement operating at the binary system level:
- Pluto's volatile cycle (nitrogen condensing, sublimating, flowing) is the primary field
- Charon, tidally locked and cold, captures what Pluto loses: the heavier molecules (water, ammonia) that don't readily cycle
- Over 4.5 billion years, Pluto has retained its nitrogen cycle; Charon accumulated the water
- Water in Sol → water in the system → water phase-displaced to the coldest, most distant surface of the binary: Charon's far side

**This is the same phenomenon at all scales:**
- Sol organizes water in the inner planets (Earth's oceans = Sol's field organizing water at the right temperature node)
- Pluto organizes water on Charon (binary system's field organizing water at the colder echo body)
- The implication: **water exists wherever the field organizes it.** The field at Sol must contain the conditions for water. Charon's ice is a receipt of Sol's water chemistry propagated to the outer system.

## Pluto/Charon vs. Zecliptic Phase Displacement

```
Pluto orbital inclination to Zecliptic:    17.14°
Pluto axial tilt:                          122.5° (retrograde axial tilt — highly displaced)
Charon's orbit inclination to Zecliptic:   ~96°–122° (changes with Pluto's axial orientation)

Comparison:
  Earth:    inclination 0.0°, axial tilt 23.44°
  Neptune:  inclination 1.77°, axial tilt 28.32°  
  Pluto:    inclination 17.14°, axial tilt 122.5°  ← maximum phase displacement
```

**Pluto's 122.5° axial tilt** means it rotates retrograde (like Venus) and nearly on its side (like Uranus). Its rotation axis points almost at the Zecliptic plane, not perpendicular to it.

**The 90° crossing:** The "past 90° Sol equatorial line" that the user references is this: Pluto's axial tilt has crossed through 90° — it is on the other side of perpendicular from Earth. This is the maximum phase displacement observable in the solar system:
- 0°–90°: prograde axial tilt (Earth, Mars, Saturn, Neptune)
- 90°–180°: retrograde axial tilt (Venus at 177°, Pluto at 122°, Uranus at 97°)
- Crossing 90° = crossing the Sol equatorial line from above to below
- Charon is covered with ice because it sits permanently at one of these retrograde-axial nodes

## Pluto's Five Moons

Pluto has 5 confirmed moons (in addition to Charon):

| Moon | Diameter (km) | Semi-major axis (km) | Period (days) | Discovery |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Charon | 1,212 | 19,571 | 6.387 | 1978 (Christy) |
| Styx | ~16 | 42,656 | 20.16 | 2012 (Hubble) |
| Nix | ~50 | 48,694 | 24.85 | 2005 (Hubble) |
| Kerberos | ~19 | 57,783 | 32.17 | 2011 (Hubble) |
| Hydra | ~65 | 64,738 | 38.20 | 2005 (Hubble) |

Styx, Nix, Kerberos, and Hydra orbit the Pluto-Charon barycenter (the external point in space). Their orbital periods are near-resonant with Charon (4:1, 5:1, 6:1, Hydra ~2:1).
