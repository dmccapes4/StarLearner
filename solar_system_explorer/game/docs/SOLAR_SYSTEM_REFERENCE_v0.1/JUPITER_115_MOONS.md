## Overview

Jupiter has **115 confirmed moons** as of April 9, 2026.  
- 8 regular (prograde, near-equatorial orbit)
- 107 irregular (captured bodies, retrograde or high-inclination)

Jupiter is the primary echo star of the solar system. Its 95→115 moon progression mirrors Sol's own history: a large body in a field organizes smaller bodies into capture orbits. The irregular moons are the solar nebula's remnants, captured when Jupiter's proto-lunar disk was still massive enough to absorb their momentum.

```
Jupiter mass:        1.898 × 10²⁷ kg  (317.8× Earth)
Jupiter radius:      69,911 km
Jupiter composition: ~75% hydrogen, ~24% helium, ~1% other  (proto-stellar composition)
Internal heat:       Jupiter radiates ~1.7× the energy it receives from Sol
                     = stellar-like internal energy generation
Zecliptic angle:     1.30° (nearly on Zecliptic — primary echo star, closely aligned)
```

| **Callisto** | J-IV | 1,882,709 | **16.6890** | 0.192° | 0.0074 | **4,821** | 1.076×10²³ |

**Galilean orbital resonance (Laplace resonance):**
```
Io : Europa : Ganymede period ratio = 1 : 2 : 4
Io completes 4 orbits for every 1 Ganymede orbit
Io completes 2 orbits for every 1 Europa orbit
This resonance drives tidal heating in Io and Europa
```

**Key properties:**

**Io** — most volcanically active body in the solar system. Tidal heating from Jupiter/Europa/Ganymede resonance. No water (all driven off by volcanic activity). Surface: sulfur compounds, silicate rock. Internal temperature: hot (tidally driven).

**Europa** — liquid water ocean beneath ~5–10 km ice shell. Most promising candidate for extraterrestrial life in the solar system. Surface: water ice. Internal heating from tidal flexing. Field-organized water at the first Galilean echo.

**Ganymede** — largest moon in the solar system (larger than Mercury). Has its own magnetic field. Water ice and rock. Possible internal ocean.

**Callisto** — furthest Galilean. Heavily cratered, ancient surface. Possible deep subsurface ocean. Not in resonance — sits outside the resonance chain.

## Inclination Distribution — Phase Displacement Map

```
Inclination to Zecliptic    Moons in this range     Group
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
0° – 5°                     8 moons                 Inner + Galilean
27° – 30°                   11 moons                Himalia group
34° – 56°                   3 moons                 Carpo + Valetudo
144° – 158°                 ~14 moons               Ananke group (retrograde)
164° – 165°                 ~24 moons               Carme group (retrograde)
141° – 158°                 ~16 moons               Pasiphae group (retrograde)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```

The **bimodal distribution** (0–5° prograde vs 144–165° retrograde) reflects two origins:
1. **Prograde (formed in place):** Regular moons coalesced from Jupiter's proto-lunar disk, aligned with Jupiter's equatorial plane and the Zecliptic
2. **Retrograde (captured):** Irregular moons were captured asteroids/planetesimals, entering at various angles and getting trapped in retrograde orbits by dynamical friction with the disk

The ~8 prograde (Galilean + inner) moons contain **99.9%** of the total moon mass.  
The ~107 irregular moons contain **0.1%** of the total moon mass.

## Jupiter as Echo Star — Phase Displacement Summary

Jupiter's moon system is a miniature solar system. The pattern mirrors the planets:
- **Inner/regular moons → aligned with field (low inclination, prograde)**
- **Outer/irregular moons → phase-displaced (high inclination, retrograde)**
- **Successively farther moons → lower speeds, greater inclination spread**

The Galilean resonance (1:2:4 Io-Europa-Ganymede) is the echo of the inner planets' solar resonances. The tidal heating of Io mirrors Sol's heating of the inner planets. Europa's sub-ice ocean is the same water-organizing field that gives Earth its oceans, echoed at the Jupiter node.
