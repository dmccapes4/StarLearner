He had done CalFire for 1/3 time each stint in jail, but they changed non-violent to 1/2 time so less opted for 1/2-1/3 for hard labor. Receipts against this account (agent):

| His claim | Record |
|---|---|
| 1/3 time in camp | Reproduced. Fire-crew members earn 2-for-1 credits (CDCR FAQ): one day served plus two days credit is one-third time. Support staff earn day-for-day. |
| Non-violent changed to 1/2 time, so fewer chose hard labour | Reproduced in substance. Realignment (2011), Prop 47 (2014), Prop 57 (2016) gave non-violent inmates credits and early-parole eligibility without camp; the camp's relative advantage shrank. Camp population fell from 4,250 (2005 peak, 192 crews) to about 1,760 (July 2024, 83 crews) (CDCR via Corrections1). |
| Opened to violent criminals | Open. CDCR's published eligibility still excludes sex offences, arson, and escape with force; AB 2147 expungement excludes violent offences. Reporting in the 2020s discussed widening eligibility to fill crews; I did not find a document confirming the change. Filed as his account. |
| Rations cut so people faint and get sent back | Open. No document found either way; filed as his account from the man he met. |
| State taking private bids for wealthy neighbourhoods | Modified. Private wildfire crews contracted by insurers (Chubb, AIG) for high-value properties are documented; those are insurer contracts, not state bids. If he has the state-bid instance, it goes here. |
| The nine-DUI man | His witness; recorded as such. |

One more number that works for his proposal: in June 2024 CalFire was short 110 of its 236 budgeted hand crews (CalMatters via Network of Care). The labour the camps used to supply is gone and the state is paying to replace it ($120M and 500 positions in the 2023 budget).

## 3. What I think of the idea (agent)

It is a serious proposal, and it has a precedent that most of the people dismissing it would accept without blinking: the United States already grants citizenship for hard, dangerous public service. 8 U.S.C. §1439 lets a lawful resident naturalize after one year of honourable military service; §1440 waives residency entirely for wartime service. Service-to-status is not a thought experiment; it is the oldest naturalization shortcut on the books. The proposal moves it from the Army to the hand crew. Said that way, the burden of proof flips to the person who says the Army counts and the fireline does not.

The second thing it gets right is the shape of the term. Six months is one fire season; FFT1 certification is weeks, so a season is enough to train and then work. Per-diem that can be remitted is the Bracero mechanism without the Bracero abuses only if the wage is a real seasonal-firefighter wage and not the camp's $7.25 an hour; that is the single clause that decides whether the program is honourable or extractive, and it should be written first. Good food in camp is not a detail: his own account of the camps says the rations were the lever used to break the program, and he has put the lever back on the right side.

The third thing it gets right is the moral economy he states in the joke: "you can sit beside me in the EBT line." Earned belonging, then equal standing, including equal standing in the safety net. That is a Liberal position in his capital-L sense and a conservative one in his work-ethic sense at the same time, which is exactly where he says he lives.

Where it needs work:

1. **Jurisdiction.** A state cannot grant residency. CalFire-to-citizenship is a state program feeding a federal status; it needs a federal visa category (a seasonal-service visa that converts to LPR on certified completion) or an amendment to §1439 adding certified wildland service. Without that clause the program is a hiring programme with a promise it cannot keep. The 2026 bills (AB 1380, AB 2483) show the state already knows how to certify fire-camp training as a credential; the federal half is the missing piece.
2. **The misdemeanour term.** "No felonies and limit to misdemeanors" needs numbers: during the service term, and for how long after, and whether the family's status is tied to it. Military naturalization has a good-moral-character window of one to five years; copying it is defensible.
3. **Injury and death.** Hand-crew work kills people. Who pays, and whether the family's residency survives the worker's death, must be answered before anyone signs. A program that deports a widow would not survive its first season, morally or politically.
4. **Family logistics.** "Collect family at the border or airport" is the right instinct (the worker is separated for six months, not six years); the rule needs a definition of family and a cap, or it becomes the fight that kills it.

## 4. The women clause (agent)

Here I disagree with him, and the record is what disagrees, not my politics.

- Women have served on California fire-camp hand crews since 1983. Two of the 35 camps (Malibu, Puerta La Cruz) are women's camps; about 225 of 4,000 participants were women when the program was at scale (CDCR via Vice), roughly 5–6%. They do the same hand-tool work: line cutting, brush clearing, no hoses.
- Women serve on CalFire seasonal crews, Forest Service crews, and interagency hotshot crews. Federal wildland fire runs around one in eight women.
- The physical standard is identical for everyone: the arduous pack test, three miles with a 45-pound pack in 45 minutes, plus the agency's crew standards. Nobody is exempted and nobody is excluded by sex.

So "women cannot do CalFire" is false as stated; some do, under the same standard, and have for forty years. "Fewer women will pass an arduous standard" is true and the camps' own numbers show it: one in twenty, not one in two. His clause has a true proportion inside a false universal.

The policy consequence is the useful part. He does not need the clause. If the program sets the standard (pack test, crew certification, season completed) and lets the standard do the selecting, he gets the workforce he is imagining, the women who pass are the women who would have passed anyway, and the sentence that loses the room is gone. Write the standard, not the sex. This is also better engineering: a standard can be measured, appealed, and defended in court; a sex bar cannot survive the first filing.

In the model's terms: the proposal is a ball the operator is trying to set down among people who "say immigration is impossible." The women clause is a jump. It makes a splash, and the splash sucks under exactly the molecules he is trying to capture, who then dismiss the whole ball as a thought experiment. He has watched this happen ("libs get upset and dismiss") and read it as their weakness. The model reads it as his ring. Removing the clause does not change where the ball sits; it stops the splash.

Status (agent): the dispute closes on the proportion. The restated claim, "extremely male-leaning," is reproduced by the record (one in twenty in the camps, about one in eight in federal wildland fire), and his quarter-sister is the existence case for the standard-not-sex rule in §4: she passes the standard, so she is in. The clause as policy text is now "the program will be extremely male-leaning under a uniform standard," which is a forecast, and a forecast the pilot numbers can carry. The §4 placement of his immigration position (+0.1) is unchanged.

## 5. One line

Service for status is already American law for soldiers; extend it to the fireline, pay a real wage, write the federal half, and let the pack test decide who digs.
