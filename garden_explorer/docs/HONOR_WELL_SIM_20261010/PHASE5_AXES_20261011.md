## 3. How a scalar per topic is computed

Each axis \(k\) is defined by a small bank of pole statements (five to ten per pole, written in plain policy language, no party names). For a transcript segment with embedding \(\mathbf{e}\):

- **stance** \(s_k = \tanh\big(\beta\,(\cos(\mathbf{e},\bar{\mathbf{u}}^+_k) - \cos(\mathbf{e},\bar{\mathbf{u}}^-_k))\big) \in [-1,1]\), where \(\bar{\mathbf{u}}^\pm_k\) are the mean pole embeddings and \(\beta\) is calibrated once so that the pole statements themselves score ±0.9;
- **salience** \(a_k = \max(0, \cos(\mathbf{e},\bar{\mathbf{u}}_k) - \tau)\), how much the segment is about topic \(k\) at all, with \(\bar{\mathbf{u}}_k\) the mean of both poles;
- a show-night's position \(p_k = \sum_i a_{ik}s_{ik}/\sum_i a_{ik}\) (salience-weighted stance), its radius \(r_k\) the salience-weighted standard deviation of \(s_{ik}\), and its agenda vector \(\mathbf{a}\) (what it talked about) kept alongside.

The embedding model is the one already in use (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`); if its stance separation proves weak in the pilot, the upgrade is a zero-shot NLI cross-encoder scoring each segment against each pole statement, same interface, better discrimination. Either way the procedure is fixed before the shows are scored and run identically on the operator's corpus.

What the scalar loses, honestly: a position like "secure border is necessary for immigration reform" is a sequence, not a point; the scalar records where it nets out (near zero) and the rationale text is stored next to it. Centrist and ambivalent both score near zero; the radius tells them apart, which is why \(r_k\) is carried per axis rather than as one number.

**8.1 The spike, clarified.** The spike at \(n=8\) is a property of the membrane's Green's function in \(n\) spatial dimensions (`kernel_field.py`): tension stops sharing load between balls when the base space has many axes. It constrains where the *physics* runs (the 2-d base), and it says nothing about how many stance axes we measure. Seven or eight is free of it.

**8.2 Seven.** Adopted. The seven are economy/safety net, immigration, healthcare, education, foreign war, abortion, guns; trade moves to the extended set (its stance on these shows is mostly carried by the economy axis and it is re-promotable). What an odd count buys is a pillar with an equal number of axes on either side when the operator wants to draw it that way; no formula uses primality, and nothing breaks if the count changes later, because every axis is a named column.

**8.3 The horseshoe as a weighting principle — agent's reading, adopted.** This is the operator's best move of the night and it changes what the horseshoe *is* in the model:

- Every molecule and every ball carries two vectors on the seven axes: a **stance** \(\mathbf{s}\in[-1,1]^7\) (where it stands) and an **attention** \(\mathbf{a}\) on the simplex (what it cares about, summing to 1). The **pillar** is \(\arg\max_k a_k\). The **concentration** \(h = a_{\max} - 1/7\) runs from 0 (cares about everything equally) to 6/7 (single issue). Order of axes carries nothing; "left or right of the pillar" is not stored, and if wanted it is derived as the sign agreement \(\operatorname{sign}(s_k s_{k^*})\).
- Distance is no longer Euclidean and no longer symmetric. From molecule \(m\) to any point \(\mathbf{y}\): \(d_m(\mathbf{y}) = \sum_k a_{mk}\,|s_{mk} - y_k|\). A molecule sees a ball through its own attention. Two molecules with the same stance and different pillars live in different landscapes, which is the formal version of "people do not care about all issues."
- **The horseshoe right changes the horseshoe's direction** becomes a measurable statement: Owens's pillar moves. The prediction is that her stance vector changes little on the seven (she stays right on abortion, guns, immigration) while her attention rotates to the hinge axes (institutional trust, Israel, in the extended set; foreign war in the seven), and under *her own* weighting the nearest ball flips from Shapiro to the left-coded balls. Decompose her trajectory into \(\Delta\mathbf{s}\) and \(\Delta\mathbf{a}\): the model predicts \(\|\Delta\mathbf{a}\| \gg \|\Delta\mathbf{s}\|\). A stance move would be a conversion; an attention move is a horseshoe. The named frame can tell them apart and the Euclidean frame could not.
- It also explains the operator's own line without a free parameter. With a mixed stance profile and low concentration (\(h\approx 0.09\) on the aggregate below), a molecule is far from every single-issue observer *under that observer's weighting*: the abortion-pillar viewer sees him left, the Ukraine-pillar viewer sees him right. "Libs call me MAGA and pubs call me a lib" is the asymmetric distance formula evaluated by two high-\(h\) observers.
- For the membrane: a molecule's attention vector is also how it falls. On the 2-d base the gradient it descends is the gradient of the ball's well weighted by \(a_m\) projected onto the base, so a molecule that cares about nothing a ball talks about does not feel the well at all. This replaces the uniform mobility \(1/(1+c_b b)\) with a direction-dependent one and is queued as a Phase 5 rule change.
