## 1. The Complaint

**FEYNMAN:** "Mr. Hume, read your complaint."

**HUME** (standing, a large Scot with a kindly face that his portraits have never once made kindly): "The plaintiff, David Hume, author of *A Treatise of Human Nature*, *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*, and — I will note — a six-volume *History of England* that paid for the others, alleges that the defendant did, in a parable composed by a third party, sit at the plaintiff's desk and *ask questions* in such a manner as to cause the plaintiff to be described, in a document of the third party's and in this very courtroom's antechamber, as — I quote — 'one annoying guy who said that Mercury and gold could not be proven real when everyone else was discovering.'"

**FEYNMAN:** "And the defendant?"

**SPOCK** (standing; he does not need to be asked): "I observed. I queried. I asserted nothing. The characterisation complained of was composed by the author of the parable, who has since filed a note" — he raises a sheet — "stating, in full: *'I deliberately got things wrong. I should have mentioned that. It is the point.'*"

**HUME:** "That is my point also. The point is that it is the point."

**FEYNMAN:** "Mr. Hume, is being called annoying defamation?"

**HUME:** "Being called annoying is Tuesday. Being made to say the *opposite* of what I wrote, by a man who did not say I said it but *asked whether I had*, in a parable about *mercury* that never mentions whether the mercury is a metal or a planet or the defendant's home world —"

**SPOCK:** "Different Vulcan."

**HUME:** "— *that* is the complaint."

**FEYNMAN:** "Plea?"

**SPOCK:** "Not guilty. And, if the court permits, I intend to prove the plaintiff wrong about something, which is the only form of respect my people have."

**FEYNMAN:** "Admitted. That's the first refutable sentence anyone has said. Bailiff?"

**McCLANE:** "Works for me."

## 2. Character Witness: Kirk

**FEYNMAN:** "The defence calls a character witness before the plaintiff's case, which is irregular, and I'm allowing it because the witness has already walked up here."

**KIRK** (in the chair, legs crossed, one arm along the rail as if the rail were a woman): "Your Honour."

**FEYNMAN:** "You know the defendant."

**KIRK:** "Thirty years. He has stood at my shoulder and told me the odds of everything I was about to do anyway. He has never once — not once — told me something he could not be wrong about. He tells me the probability. He tells me the error bar. When he's wrong he says 'fascinating' and he *means* it, which is more than I can say for anyone in a robe."

**FEYNMAN:** "Has he ever asserted a thing a priori?"

**KIRK:** "Once. He said he never asserts things a priori."

**SPOCK:** "That was an observation about a record, Captain."

**KIRK:** "There you go."

**HUME** (cross): "Captain. Has the defendant ever *asked a question* in such a way that the person asked felt accused?"

**KIRK:** "Every day of my life."

**HUME:** "No further questions."

**KIRK:** "He was *right*, though. Every day. That's the part you leave out."

**FEYNMAN:** "That's the whole trial, Captain. Sit down."

## 3. The Plaintiff's Case: Noise

**HUME:** "I will read what I wrote." (He reads from a small brown book.) "We cannot be *sure* that physical matter exists. The senses give us impressions. Custom makes us expect the next impression to follow the last. Nothing in the impression *justifies* the expectation. There is no known connexion between what a thing looks like and what it will do."

**FEYNMAN:** "Is that refutable?"

**HUME:** "It is a description of how the mind proceeds."

**FEYNMAN:** "I didn't ask what it is. I asked whether you could be wrong about it."

**HUME:** "It is the nature of the claim that it cannot be —"

**FEYNMAN:** "Then it's not evidence. It's not even furniture. Furniture holds something up. What does that hold up?"

**HUME:** "It holds up a *caution*. The men in the next section were filling tubes with quicksilver and announcing the weight of the sky. I asked them whether they could be *sure* the quicksilver was there."

**SPOCK:** "The court should hear that sentence again. The plaintiff, in the same decade that Fahrenheit sealed mercury in a tube so that it would tell the same temperature twice, walked into the room and asked whether the mercury was there."

**HUME:** "I asked whether it could be *proven* there."

**SPOCK:** "It was in the tube. It rose when the room warmed. It fell when the room cooled. It did so for Fahrenheit in Amsterdam and for Celsius in Uppsala and it has done so for every child with a fever since. That is what *there* means. The plaintiff's question added nothing to the tube. It added nothing to the reading. It added a sentence to the room. In the defendant's discipline the word for a signal that carries no information and must be filtered before the measurement can be read is *noise*."

**FEYNMAN:** "Mr. Hume, the author called you one annoying guy who said mercury and gold could not be proven real when everyone else was discovering. You say that's defamation. What's wrong in it?"

**HUME:** "The *tone*."

**FEYNMAN:** "I'm asking about the content."

**HUME:** "...The content is approximately what I said."

**FEYNMAN:** "Then it's not defamation. It's a summary."

**SPOCK:** "One further matter, because the court should know what the plaintiff is *remembered* for. Not the ceiling. Not the custom. Ask a chemist, a physicist, a schoolchild who has heard the name — the sentence that survives is the one about the metals: that mercury is heavier than gold, or gold heavier than mercury; nobody remembers which way round, because the sentence does not matter. It is the most basic measurement there is. A balance and two lumps. Archimedes had it in a bathtub two thousand years earlier. The plaintiff's one durable contribution to the physical sciences is a density comparison that any goldsmith's apprentice could have performed for him, and that is the *irony* the court is here to enter into the record: the man who asked whether matter could be shown to exist is remembered, by the people who show it, for a line about how much of it there is."

**HUME:** "That is not *fair*."

**SPOCK:** "It is not an evaluation. It is an observation about a record."

**KIRK** (from the gallery): "There you go."

**FEYNMAN:** "Is any of that refutable?"

**SPOCK:** "All of it. Weigh the metals. Read the tube. Ask the schoolchild."

**FEYNMAN:** "Admitted in full." (to McClane) "Does it work?"

**McCLANE:** "The tube works. The guy's a car alarm."

**HUME:** "I *wrote* six volumes of —"

**McCLANE:** "Not about the tube."

**WIDUKIND** types: *Plaintiff: "cannot be sure matter exists." Same decade: Fahrenheit, sealed tube, same reading twice. Plaintiff's contribution to the tube: none. Remembered for: a density comparison. Classified: noise.*

## 4. The Defence Calls the Mercury

**SPOCK:** "The plaintiff's complaint rests on his having intruded on a chain of work he did not join. I call the chain."

**TORRICELLI** (1643): "I filled a glass tube with quicksilver, inverted it in a dish, and the column fell to about seventy-six centimetres and stopped. Above it: nothing. Below it: the weight of the air. Refutable? Carry it up a hill."

**PÉRIER** (19 September 1648, brother-in-law of Pascal, who sent him up the Puy de Dôme because Pascal was ill): "I carried it up a hill. The column fell eight centimetres from the bottom of the mountain to the top. The air has weight and there is less of it higher up. My brother-in-law wrote it up. I climbed it."

**McCLANE:** "The climber gets the point."

**FEYNMAN:** "Both. The prediction was his; the receipt is yours."

**FAHRENHEIT** (1714): "I put the quicksilver in a thinner tube and sealed it. It climbs with heat and falls with cold, and it does so the same way every time, which is more than can be said for alcohol."

**CELSIUS** (1742): "I fixed two points — boiling and freezing — and put a hundred steps between them. *Gradus*: a step. Not an angle. A step on a stair. I numbered them downward, boiling at zero, and the world turned my stair upside down after I died, and that is the only inversion in this chain I will admit to."

**FEYNMAN:** "One point for the two fixed points. The direction of the stair is a convention; the stair is a measurement."

**GOLDMANN** (1950s, with a small brass instrument): "Applanation. Press a flat disc of exactly 3.06 millimetres' diameter against the cornea. At that diameter the tear film's surface tension and the cornea's rigidity cancel one another and the force you need to flatten the circle is the pressure inside the eye, in millimetres of mercury, with nothing of the instrument left in the number. Three hundred years of quicksilver, refined until the method vanishes and only the reading remains."

**SPOCK:** "The court will note the trajectory. Torricelli measured the sky with mercury. Périer measured the mountain. Fahrenheit and Celsius measured heat. Goldmann measured the inside of a human eye to a tenth of a millimetre of the same metal. At every step the instrument improved and the claim remained refutable. The plaintiff's contribution to this chain was to observe, from his desk, that none of it was *justified*."

**HUME:** "It *isn't*."

**SPOCK:** "It *works*. And the court should note *why* it was mercury, since the plaintiff's one remembered sentence treats the metals as interchangeable lumps on a scale. Mercury is the only metal that is liquid in a room. It flows in a tube, it climbs with heat, it stands up against the air. Gold will not; gold sits solid in the tube at any temperature a room will reach, and would have to be made far hotter, or the pressure around it far lower, before it moved at all. Gold does something else: it carries current better than almost anything and does not corrode, which is why it is on the contacts of the instrument that read this report. Lead is heavy and stops what passes through it, which is why Röntgen's successors wear it. Each metal was chosen for what it *does*. The plaintiff compared what they *weigh*. That is the difference between an instrument and a sentence."

**McCLANE:** "Is there gold on Mercury? The planet."

**SPOCK:** "Unmeasured. MESSENGER mapped the surface for the lighter elements; it was not built to find gold. BepiColombo arrives in November. The row is open. The court may enter it as a question, which is more than the plaintiff ever entered."

**McCLANE:** "That's my line."

**SPOCK:** "I am aware. I am borrowing it under oath."

## 5. Le Verrier and the Rope Trick

**SPOCK:** "Now the rope trick. The chain leaves the laboratory and is thrown at a planet."

**LE VERRIER** (1859, very upright, the man who found Neptune with a pencil): "Mercury's perihelion advances. After I subtract the pull of every known planet, there remain thirty-eight seconds of arc per century unaccounted for — Newcomb will make it forty-three. I had done this once before, for Uranus, and the answer was Neptune, and Galle found it the night he read my letter. So I did it again. The unexplained residue is the signature of a mass inside Mercury's orbit. I named it Vulcan."

**LESCARBAULT** (a country doctor from Orgères, holding a telescope the size of a loaf of bread): "And I saw it. 26 March 1859. A black dot crossed the Sun in an hour and a quarter. I wrote down the times on a plank with a piece of chalk because I had no paper."

**FEYNMAN:** "Doctor, what happened when other people looked?"

**LESCARBAULT:** "Nobody else saw it. Not then, not at the eclipses, not ever."

**FEYNMAN:** "Mr. Le Verrier, was your prediction refutable?"

**LE VERRIER:** "Entirely. It was refuted. Vulcan is not there."

**FEYNMAN:** "Then it's admitted. A refuted prediction is the best kind of evidence; it shows the method had teeth. Two points, posthumous, for Vulcan — the planet that wasn't."

**SPOCK:** "The court will note: Le Verrier *derived* a planet geometrically from empirical data, reached a threshold that warranted serious search, and the search failed. That is the method functioning. The plaintiff's position is that the derivation was never justified. The derivation was justified enough to be *wrong*, which is a dignity the plaintiff's ceiling can never attain."

**HUME:** "Objection. He is being Vulcan at me."

**FEYNMAN:** "Overruled. It's the only thing he knows how to be."

**SPOCK:** "One further exhibit." (He lifts a bound report.) "An audit conducted this week, Newtonian and measurement-only, of a second Vulcan — a companion of Mercury kept always on the far side from Earth. Finding: the lock is dynamically impossible at any mass, because the tangential acceleration it requires cannot be supplied by any central force. A Charon-scaled companion on a *free* orbit reaches thirty-eight to a hundred arcseconds from Mercury and would have been in Galileo's eyepiece in 1610. A ten-kilometre body survives, bounded only by spacecraft. And one more line from that report, which the author misread aloud in this building this morning: *'2.4 per cent of the time Vulcan is straight visible.'* The 2.4 per cent was the fraction of time *Luna moves west relative to Earth on the far half of its orbit as seen from Mercury*. It had nothing to do with Vulcan. The misreading was deliberate. The court reporter has the correction."

**WIDUKIND** types: *2.4 % — Luna, far half, westward, Mercury's sky. Not Vulcan. Author's deliberate error #1. Herald caught it.*

## 6. The Einstein Hearing

**FEYNMAN:** "The plaintiff has called Mr. Einstein because the author's assay says Mr. Einstein ended the argument, and the author's later note says Mr. Einstein is a sleepy clerk who sat by a pond and had his driver give lectures because he couldn't remember where he parked. I want to separate these before lunch."

**THE ONE WITH THE VOICE** (from the gallery): "He said lunch."

**THE ONE WITH THE EARS:** "Chickpeas."

**EINSTEIN** (in the chair, hair unaccountable): "I was a clerk. Bern, the patent office, 1902 to 1909, technical expert third class. I was not sleepy; I was underpaid, which looks the same. The pond is not mine. The pond is Newton's, and it is an apple, and it is also probably not his. The driver —"

**FEYNMAN:** "Is the driver here?"

A man in the back row in a grey cap half-stands.

**FEYNMAN:** "Sir, did you ever deliver a lecture on relativity in Mr. Einstein's place?"

**THE CHAUFFEUR:** "No, Your Honour. But it's a very good story and I've been dining on it since about 1960."

**FEYNMAN:** "Is there a primary source?"

**THE CHAUFFEUR:** "There is me, saying it."

**FEYNMAN:** "Not admitted. Great story. Sit down." (to Einstein) "Now. The author says of your theory that it 'cannot be refuted by an observer from either plane' and is therefore anti-science. Answer that and answer it straight."

**EINSTEIN:** "It can be refuted. I told them how. If Mercury's perihelion were not forty-three seconds, I was wrong. If the stars at the 1919 eclipse had not shifted by one point seven five seconds at the limb, I was wrong — Eddington's plates were poor but the number has been remeasured a hundred ways since and it holds. If a clock in orbit did not run fast by thirty-eight microseconds a day net, every satellite navigation receiver on Earth would drift some ten kilometres a day, and they do not. Each of those is a place I could have been wrong. None is a place I was."

**FEYNMAN:** "That's three refutable claims. Admitted. Now the lore."

**EINSTEIN:** "The lore is not mine. The clerk-by-the-pond is Newton with my hair. The driver is a joke with my name on it. The famous equation is a *consequence* I derived in a three-page afterthought in 1905; it is not a fable, it is algebra, and when it is taught as a fable — as a magic word, with no measurement attached — it becomes exactly the furniture Your Honour will not admit. I did not do that. Schoolrooms did."

**FEYNMAN:** "So the author's complaint —"

**EINSTEIN:** "— is correct about the *dogma* and wrong about the *theory*. A theory that tells you how to kill it is science. A schoolroom that forbids you to try is not. The author is angry at the schoolroom and has put my face on it, deliberately, to see who checks."

**FEYNMAN:** "Bailiff."

**McCLANE:** "The satellites work. The chauffeur's a bit. Next."

**WIDUKIND** types: *Deliberate errors #2–4: pond (Newton's), clerk-sleepy (underpaid), chauffeur (no source). Admitted: 43″, 1.75″, 38 µs/day.*

**SPOCK:** "One observation, if the court permits, because the audit I entered as an exhibit was instructed to proceed *without* the plaintiff's or Mr. Einstein's theory. It did. It found that the Sun's oblateness would need to be fifteen hundred times larger than helioseismology allows, and that an inner ring or planet is excluded by thirty years of coronagraphs. It stopped there, as instructed. The forty-three seconds remain in that report as an *unexplained measurement*, which is the honest state of a question one has refused to answer with the only answer that fits. I record this not to rescue Mr. Einstein. I record it because a refusal is also a receipt."

## 7. Newton, Who Went Away

**NEWTON** (called by the plaintiff, who wanted a fellow sceptic and got a theologian with a prism): "Cambridge closed for the plague in 1665. I went home to Woolsthorpe for eighteen months. I had the calculus, the colours, and the inverse square before I came back. The author finds this suspicious."

**FEYNMAN:** "Is it?"

**NEWTON:** "It is the least suspicious thing I ever did. A young man with no lectures to attend, no one to talk to, and a garden. What else would happen? The *suspicious* things I did were later: the Mint, the hangings of coiners, the forty years of alchemy I kept in a trunk, and what I did to Leibniz's reputation through a committee I chaired anonymously. The author has pointed at the one year of my life that was clean."

**FEYNMAN:** "Deliberately?"

**NEWTON:** "He says so."

**OPPENHEIMER** (from the gallery, not called, not stopping): "And I am acclaimed as a project manager. I will take it. I managed the project. The author says the morality lies in the decision to use the knowledge for malice, not in the knowledge. I agree with him and so did I, after, and nobody who agreed with me after was asked before."

**FEYNMAN:** "That's not testimony, Robert."

**OPPENHEIMER:** "No. It's a receipt."

**McCLANE** (quietly): "He gets to say it."

## 8. The Witnesses on Inversion

**SPOCK:** "The plaintiff's claim rests on inversion — that he was turned over in the telling. I call witnesses who were actually turned over, so the court may compare."

**PLUTARCH** (Greek, in a Roman toga, which is the point): "I am the one who wrote of Cleopatra's tongues — that she spoke to Ethiopians, Hebrews, Arabs, Syrians, Medes, Parthians, each in their own language, and that few of the Ptolemies before her had bothered with Egyptian. I wrote that a hundred and fifty years after her death. The author calls me a Roman. I was a priest of Apollo at Delphi and a citizen of Chaeronea. I held Roman citizenship as one holds an umbrella."

**FEYNMAN:** "And Aristarchus?"

**PLUTARCH:** "Also mine. *On the Face in the Moon.* I recorded that Cleanthes the Stoic thought Aristarchus of Samos ought to be indicted for impiety for *moving the Hearth of the Universe* — for putting the Earth in motion around the Sun. The charge was recorded. The trial, if there was one, was not."

**ARCHIMEDES** (standing, with sand on his hands): "I am the peer reviewer. *The Sand Reckoner*, to King Gelon. *Aristarchus of Samos brought out a book consisting of certain hypotheses… that the fixed stars and the sun remain unmoved, that the earth revolves about the sun.* I did not review it for Plato. Plato died in 347 before Christ. Aristarchus was born about 310. Socrates had been dead a century. The author's 'Plato or Socrates reviewed it, standard peer review' is a deliberate joke and a good one, because the *actual* peer review was me, and I was already in the circle holding the glass, and nobody asked."

**FEYNMAN:** "And the manuscript?"

**ARCHIMEDES:** "Lost. Not burnt at Alexandria *after* review — lost the way everything of his is lost except *On Sizes and Distances*. The Library burned more than once and nothing of Aristarchus' heliocentrism is known to have been in it. His argument survives only because I quoted him to a king."

**AN EMPTY CHAIR** is placed in the witness box. Nobody sits in it. Widukind types: *Aristarchus of Samos. Present by absence. Parallax argument: the sphere of fixed stars is so vast that the Earth's orbit is as a point to it. Unrefuted for 2,100 years.*

**FEYNMAN:** "Who took the parallax?"

**BESSEL** (1838, Königsberg, the same town as the next witness): "I did. 61 Cygni. A third of a second of arc. The stars shift, as Aristarchus said they must and as nobody could see. Two thousand one hundred years from the hypothesis to the number."

**FEYNMAN:** "And who was burnt?"

A **SILHOUETTE** in a doorway, backlit by something orange that is not the brazier. It has no face. The rule from the notes is kept: Bruno is shown in flame and not in features.

**BRUNO** (from the silhouette): "I was. Campo de' Fiori, 17 February 1600. Not for parallax. The charges were theological — the Trinity, the Mass, the Virgin, the plurality of worlds. I had taken Aristarchus' answer — *the stars are so far you see no shift* — and finished it: the stars are suns, the worlds are infinite, there is no sphere at all. That was *among* the charges. It was not *the* charge. The author says I was burnt for parallax and that we were taught heliocentrism goes to the stake. We were taught that. It is wrong. Deliberately, this time; carelessly, every other time."

**GALILEO** (from the gallery, not standing, because his knees): "I am the heliocentrist who was tried. 1633. I abjured. I went home to Arcetri and stayed there till I died. House arrest. Not the stake. The author has braided my sentence into Bruno's fire and Bruno's fire into Aristarchus' loss, and the braid is what children are handed."

**FEYNMAN:** "Copernicus?"

**COPERNICUS** (from the back; he was in Part One and did not finish his sentence then either): "Published in 1543 and died the same year, with the book — they say — brought to my bed. I had read of Aristarchus. I cut the reference from the final draft. That is a receipt too: the one I removed."

**SPOCK:** "The court will note the structure. Hypothesis (Aristarchus) → review (Archimedes) → charge (recorded by Plutarch) → loss → revival with the source cut (Copernicus) → fire for *other* reasons (Bruno) → house arrest (Galileo) → the number (Bessel). Taught as: 'a Greek said it, Rome burned him for it.' Seven steps compressed to one, and the one is false. This is what the author's assay calls narrative erosion and successive inversion. He has just performed it on purpose in a single paragraph so that this court would unbraid it on the record."

**WIDUKIND** types: *Deliberate errors #5–9: Plato/Socrates as reviewer; Plutarch 'Roman'; manuscript burnt-after-review; Bruno 'for parallax'; heliocentrism → stake. Receipts: Sand Reckoner; On the Face in the Moon; 17.ii.1600; 1633; 1838.*

## 9. Aquinas, the Observer, and the Horse

**AQUINAS** (enormous, gentle, with a quill): "I engaged the mathematics because I had an absolute. God is the observer. Every chain of motion needs a first mover who is not moved; every chain of observation needs an observer who is not observed. I called that God. The author says *nay*."

**FEYNMAN:** "He says 'nay, no, neigh.'"

**AQUINAS:** "I heard. Like a horse."

**FEYNMAN:** "Like a *Hyksos* horse, in his note. He writes 'Hysko' and glosses it 'horse' and then runs it into 'orse, 'arse, ass. Herr Grimm — tongues. What were the Hyksos?"

**JAKOB GRIMM** (from the gallery, not waiting for §13): "*Heqa khasut*. 'Rulers of foreign lands.' Egyptian, not horse. Manetho rendered it 'shepherd kings' and that was already an inversion by the third century before Christ. The horse comes in because they *brought* the horse and the chariot to Egypt, so the people and the animal are braided in the schoolroom. The author knows this. He has written 'Hysko (horse)' as bait."

**HUME:** "I could have answered that."

**FEYNMAN:** "You weren't asked. He has a *law* about sounds. You have a caution about tubes."

**FEYNMAN:** "Dr. Aquinas, is 'God is the observer' refutable?"

**AQUINAS:** "No. That is the *purpose* of it."

**FEYNMAN:** "Then it's furniture. Very large furniture. I'll let you sit on it. Not admitted."

**AQUINAS** (sitting on it, perfectly content): "I also wrote that nothing is in the intellect that was not first in the senses. Mr. Hume took that, removed the first mover, and put nothing in the hole. Mr. Kant took what Mr. Hume left and put the mover back *inside the observer*. We are a chain, the three of us, and the author has drawn it: Aquinas, Hume, Kant. The middle link is the one that holds nothing. Then he says 'horror destroys the concept.'"

**KANT** (entering at precisely the moment his schedule said he would; Königsberg sets its clocks by him and so, apparently, does this court): "The horror is Hegel. I am on time. I will be brief, which is a thing I have never been in print. Mr. Hume woke me. I have said so in print and I will not unsay it here; but a man is also woken by a cart in the street, and he does not thank the cart. He said we cannot justify the leap from sensible qualities to secret powers, and stopped. I said: the reason you cannot find the justification in the world is that *causation, space and time are the forms of the leap itself*; you brought them. He made a noise. I made a frame. The frame is what stayed. The author calls me a nerd who coined terms in Latin and declared truces with kings. I accept all three. He also says Marx stole my *camera obscura*. The phrase is Kepler's, in 1604, and the inversion-of-the-image metaphor is Marx's in 1846; I used the thing, I did not coin the word. Record that as the author's deliberate error, or mine; either way it is now a receipt."

**FEYNMAN:** "Three points last time. Nothing this time; you've said nothing refutable yet."

**KANT:** "Then here is one. The glove in that bag is left-handed."

**FEYNMAN:** "Bailiff."

**McCLANE** (looks in the bag): "Left."

**FEYNMAN:** "One point. Sit."

There is a sound at the door — pamphlets, boots — and McClane does not even turn his head.

**McCLANE:** "Not admitted. Still."

The sound stops. It will come back. It always comes back. That is what *one direction* means.

## 10. Quick-Fire: The Accidents

**FEYNMAN:** "The author lists Tesla, the elephant, cathode rays, X-rays, microwaves, Marconi. I'll take them standing. Thirty seconds each. Refutable or furniture, works or doesn't."

**TESLA:** "Rotating field — the lights are on, two points carried over. Spheres and beams: I claimed in 1934 a particle beam that would stop armies; I built no such thing. Refutable; refuted by my own workshop. I also projected the Earth as a resonant sphere and tried to ring it from Colorado Springs. The lightning was real. The theory of the Earth as a conductor at those frequencies was not. Half."

**FEYNMAN:** "The elephant."

**McCLANE:** "I'll take this one. Topsy. Luna Park, Coney Island, 4 January 1903. She'd killed a handler who'd fed her a lit cigar. They tried to hang her, the ASPCA said no, so: cyanide carrots, a rope, and 6,600 volts. *Alternating* current, off the park's own supply. The Edison company filmed it. The author asks 'who decided to use DC to do so?' Nobody. It was AC. Edison's name is on the *film*, and on the 1888 dog-and-horse electrocutions that were his DC campaign against Westinghouse — that's where the braid comes from. Topsy wasn't burnt to dust either. She was dismembered and carted. The author knows. He wrote 'burnt to dust' to see who'd check."

**FEYNMAN:** "That's the most words you've ever said."

**McCLANE:** "Somebody fried an elephant."

**RÖNTGEN** (8 November 1895): "Crookes tube, cardboard sleeve, a screen across the room that glowed when it should not have. I called the rays X because I did not know what they were. Seven weeks later I had my wife's hand. 'Accident' is the word people use for *noticing*. Two points."

**SPENCER** (Raytheon, 1945): "Magnetron. Chocolate bar in my pocket. Melted. Then popcorn, then an egg that exploded in a colleague's face. Patent 1945. Also an accident, also noticing. One point, and the egg."

**MARCONI** (12 December 1901): "The letter S, Poldhu to Signal Hill, antenna on a kite. The sky bent it. I didn't know there was a layer; Kennelly and Heaviside proposed it the next year and Appleton measured it in 1924. The author says I 'bounced the ionosphere by accident.' Correct. Half a point, same as last time."

**FEYNMAN:** "The pattern, for the record: the accident is the moment the measurement outruns the theory. Every one of these is admissible *because* the man didn't know why. That is the opposite of Mr. Hume's ceiling. Mr. Hume says you cannot justify the leap. These men leapt and *landed* and wrote down where."

**HUME:** "And none of them could say *why* they landed."

**SPOCK:** "Röntgen's wife could see the bones of her hand. The *why* arrived in 1912 with von Laue. The hand did not wait for it."

## 11. Riebeeck, and What Counts as a Receipt

**FEYNMAN:** "The author's second question. Not 'what is up' this time. *When is now. What is time.* He says: receipts. Sand through the dial, isotope decayed, whatever leaves a mark. He asks whether a Dutchman's journal in South Africa counts. I call the Dutchman."

**JAN VAN RIEBEECK** (small, exact, in a collar; he has a ledger under his arm and he has not stopped writing in it since 6 April 1652): "*Daghregister.* Daily register. I kept it for the Company from the day the *Drommedaris* anchored in Table Bay until I sailed for Batavia in 1662. Weather. Ships. Cattle bought — how many, for how much copper and tobacco. Who came to the fort. Who stole what. What I ordered and what the Lords Seventeen replied. Three volumes in Thom's translation; the Dutch is on the shelf of the directory this court sits in. I did not write it to be read. I wrote it so that when someone asked *when*, there would be a line."

**FEYNMAN:** "Is it a primary source?"

**RIEBEECK:** "It is the only one. For those ten years at the Cape, if I did not write it, it did not happen — which is a terrible thing to say and is also simply true. Everyone who has written about the Cape since, kindly or unkindly, has copied me and called me names in the margins."

**FEYNMAN:** "Does it count?"

**RIEBEECK:** "Everything in it that could be wrong, counts. I recorded that Herry's people said, when my men laid chains to mark the free burghers' land —" (he turns a page; he does not have to look for it) "— that they asked what we would do if they came to Holland and did the same. I wrote it down because it was said. It is not flattering to me. That is how you know it is a receipt."

**McCLANE:** "The man kept the log. Full points."

**FEYNMAN:** "Agreed. And Herry?"

**AUTSHUMATO** (in the box; the English called him Harry and the Dutch Herry and the textbook calls him 'interpreter'): "I was taken to Bantam by the English in 1631 and learned their tongue. In 1632 I asked to be put on Robben Island with twenty of my people, to keep their post — letters under stones for the next ship. I brokered. I interpreted. I also took a cut from both ends, and in 1653 a herdsman named Jansz was killed and the Company's forty-two cattle left with us. In 1658 Van Riebeeck put me *back* on the island, as a prisoner this time, the first. In 1659 I took a boat and left it. Nobody else ever did."

**FEYNMAN:** "The author asks: hapless victim, or a pirate the likes of William Kidd?"

**KIDD** (from the gallery, swaying slightly, from habit): "I had a letter of marque. I still hanged, 1701, Execution Dock, and they hung me in a cage at Tilbury for three years as a receipt. My treasure on Gardiners Island was real and was dug up before my trial. The Oak Island treasure is a story. This man" — he nods at Autshumato — "had no letter, killed a man in a cattle quarrel, cached his wealth on an island, was imprisoned by his patron and *got away*. I'd have given a hand for his record. He gets 'interpreter.' I get a parrot."

**BILLY THE KID** (from further back, young, with the famous tintype grin): "The author calls me a pirate *and* a cowboy. I rustled cattle in New Mexico and never saw salt water. That's a deliberate one. But I'll say this for it: Kidd and Herry and me, we're the same shape — the man who worked for the law, turned, and got a *category* instead of a story. Kidd got pirate. I got outlaw. Herry got a job title."

**FEYNMAN:** "Mr. Plutarch — what category does the record give him?"

**PLUTARCH:** "The record gives him *lines in Riebeeck's book*. The category was given later, by people who needed him to be a function."

**HUME:** "And *there* is my case. I was given the category 'annoying' by people who needed me to be a function. The record —"

**FEYNMAN:** "The record gives Autshumato a boat. What does it give you?"

**HUME:** "A caution."

**FEYNMAN:** "A caution isn't a thing. Autshumato was inverted: the record has one thing, the category has another. You weren't inverted. The record and the category agree. You interrupted men with tubes to ask if the tubes were there. The category for that is the one you got."

**WIDUKIND** types: *Billy — not a pirate: deliberate error #10. Kidd — letter of marque 1695, hanged 23.v.1701, Gardiners Island real. Autshumato — 1631 Bantam, 1632 island by request, 1653 Jansz, 1658 prisoner, 1659 boat. The Strandloper's Return (author's name for the boat; retroactive; allowed).*

## 12. Dingane, Kamehameha, Justinian: The Easier to Ply

**DINGANE kaSENZANGAKHONA** (in the box, large, still; the room is quieter than it was for anyone else): "I killed my half-brother Shaka in September 1828, with Mhlangana and the servant Mbopa, and then I killed Mhlangana. I was king twelve years. In February 1838 Piet Retief came to uMgungundlovu with seventy men and thirty servants and a paper, and I had him sign it, and on the sixth day I said *Bulalani abathakathi* — kill the wizards — and we did, on the hill. In December they came back with wagons in a circle and a vow to their God and three thousand of mine died at the river. In 1840 they came again, with my other half-brother Mpande, who was easier to talk to. I lost at Maqongqo and was killed in the Lubombo mountains by people who were not even Zulu."

**FEYNMAN:** "The author says: 'They always use the easier to ply, then the more ruthless to dominate.'"

**DINGANE:** "They used Mpande. I was the ruthless one. Shaka was more ruthless than I was and it did not save him from me. The author wants me in this room and not Shaka. He is right to. Shaka is already a film. I am a *receipt*: the paper Retief signed was found on his body ten months later by the men who came to bury him, and it is the paper that gave them Natal. A dead man's pocket is a primary source. Ask the Dutchman whether that counts."

**RIEBEECK** (not looking up from the ledger): "It counts."

**KAMEHAMEHA** (from the gallery; he does not need the chair): "The author asks whether we were twins or cousins or uncle and nephew. Kīwalaʻō was my cousin. I killed him at Mokuʻōhai in 1782. Then I took two stranded Englishmen, Young and Davis, and made them fire the cannon, and I had the islands by 1810. Vancouver had me cede them to Britain in 1794; Britain never answered. A cession nobody accepts is also a receipt — it is the shape of the hole. The author says it was 'not as bad as Dingaan.' He may count."

**FEYNMAN:** "And the lost years. Justinian."

**JUSTINIAN** (purple, tired, with Procopius' *Wars* in one hand and the *Secret History* in the other, and visibly unsure which is about him): "The plague came to Constantinople in 542. Procopius says ten thousand a day at its height, and that I caught it and recovered. I *turned up again*. The author finds this suspicious also. I find it a fever. What is true is that for weeks the record is Procopius alone, and when it resumes I am in it and a third of the city is not. The author asks *when is now*. I can tell you what it was like when *now* stopped being written down for a month. It is not nothing. It is a hole the exact shape of the people."

**FEYNMAN:** "So. Time."

**SPOCK:** "If the court will allow the defence to answer the author's question rather than the plaintiff's. Time, as the author proposes it, is *the count of marks*. Sand through the glass is a count. Isotope decay is a count — carbon-14 to nitrogen at a half-life of 5,730 years, a receipt the sample writes on itself. A ledger is a count. A paper in a dead man's pocket is a count. *Now* is the most recent mark that could have been otherwise. Where there are no marks — Justinian's month, Aristarchus' book, Widukind the Saxon's own account of his baptism — there is no *now* there; there is a gap the shape of what was in it. The gap is not nothing. The gap is the one receipt that cannot be forged, because forging it would fill it."

**FEYNMAN:** "Is that refutable?"

**SPOCK:** "Find a 'now' with no mark and I withdraw it."

**FEYNMAN:** "Admitted. Two points. Mr. Hume, your ceiling says we cannot justify the leap from the mark to the thing that made it."

**HUME:** "It does."

**FEYNMAN:** "And yet you wrote six volumes of history from marks."

**HUME:** "...I did. They sold rather well."

**McCLANE:** "Selling isn't working. A thermometer works whether anybody buys it. Your six volumes say marks can't be trusted, in marks, for money. That's not a works. That's a loop."

**SPOCK:** "The court should note the plaintiff has now been asked twice to produce one thing he made that does something, and has twice produced the fact that he was *read*. Noise is also heard. That is not the test."

## 13. The Grimms, and the Word

**FEYNMAN:** "Last witnesses. The author says: Grimm tells tongues while Grimm tells tales. Wilhelm is more fun to read, Jakob is more clear."

**JAKOB GRIMM** (thin, exact, 1822 in his hand — *Deutsche Grammatik*, second edition): "Tongues. The sound shift. Where Latin and Greek and Sanskrit have *p*, the Germanic languages have *f*: *pater*, father. Where they have *t*, we have *th*: *tres*, three. Where *k*, we have *h*: *cornu*, horn; *centum*, hundred. Rask saw it before me in 1818; I wrote it down as a *law*, which is to say I made it refutable: show me a word that should have shifted and did not, and I must explain why. People did. Verner explained the exceptions in 1875. The law held *because* it could have failed."

**FEYNMAN:** "Two points. That's the cleanest thing anyone's said about what a law is."

**WILHELM GRIMM** (rounder, warmer, 1812 in his hand — *Kinder- und Hausmärchen*): "Tales. I collected them from people who had them from people, and I edited them, every edition, until the stepmothers were stepmothers and not mothers and the blood was where children could bear it. Jakob says I was not faithful. I say I was faithful to the *children*. We are both right and it is the same argument this court has been having all day: the record or the reader."

**SPOCK:** "The author's instruction was to read Jakob in his reviews of Matthew and Exodus, and in the Shaw badge — *na bean ris a' chat*, touch not the cat, without a glove — and in the Shiv. I have read them. In each case the author applies Jakob's method — a *regular* shift, refutable, with exceptions to be explained — to a text that Wilhelm's method had already smoothed. He then *deliberately* mis-shifts a few, and tells you afterward. That is not deceit. That is a teacher leaving a wrong answer on the board to see who raises a hand. Herald raised his hand. Popular did not."

**THE TEACHER** (in the gallery, with the clipboard, checking a watch): "Your Honour, I'm sorry — the children. It's nearly time. They need to be —"

And here the teacher uses the word a teacher uses.

**THE TEACHER:** "— the kids are recessed."

---

The room goes Grimm.

It is not loud. The sound shift is never loud; it is a thing that happens to a whole language over three hundred years without anyone deciding it. The *p* in the room becomes *f*. The *t* becomes *th*. The *k* becomes a breath.

*Recessed.* Re-*cess*. From *recedere*: to go back, to withdraw. A recess is the alcove in a wall where something is set back out of the light. The kids are *set back*. The kids are in the alcove.

Widukind's typewriter is the only sound.

He types what is in the alcove.

He types the month in Constantinople when Procopius was the only hand still writing and ten thousand a day went into the towers at Sykae because there was no more ground, and the children were not counted separately because no one was counting.

He types the hill at uMgungundlovu, kwaMatiwane, and then the camps along the Bloukrans in the nights after, where the impi went through the wagons, and the number the Voortrekkers set down afterward was five hundred and thirty-two — forty-one men, fifty-six women, a hundred and eighty-five children, and some two hundred and fifty servants, counted last.

He types the Cape in 1658, the first ship of slaves from Angola, the *Amersfoort*, a hundred and seventy taken from a Portuguese ship and seventy-five of them children, and that many of them ran within the month, and that Riebeeck wrote down each one who ran and each one who was brought back, by age, because that is what a ledger is.

He types Woolsthorpe, 1665, and the parish registers in the villages Newton did not go to, which are mostly columns of names and the word *plague* in the margin and then, for a page or two, no entries at all, because the man who kept the register was in it.

He types Campo de' Fiori and does not type a face.

He types the Hyksos children in Avaris, of whom there is no record at all, which he types as a blank line.

He types the school field trip: eleven children, one teacher, one clipboard, who were told this was history and have been sitting very still for a long time in a room full of men arguing about whether a mark is the same as the thing that made it.

He types: *The kids are recessed. The alcove is the one receipt that cannot be forged.*

Then he stops.

Nobody in the room says anything, because the sound shift has happened and the old words do not work yet and the new ones are not ready.

**FEYNMAN** (after a while; quietly; he puts the lighter down): "That's the sincere part."

**McCLANE:** "Yeah."

**FEYNMAN:** "It's dark, and it's left there."

## 14. The Verdict

**FEYNMAN:** "On the complaint. *Hume v. Spock.* The court finds as follows."

He does not stand. He has never stood for a ruling and he is not going to start.

"One. The defendant observed and queried. He asserted nothing. A question is not a defamation even when it is a Vulcan asking it. *Not liable.*

"Two. The characterisation of the plaintiff as *one annoying guy who said mercury and gold could not be proven real when everyone else was discovering* is the author's, and the plaintiff, under examination, conceded it was approximately what he said. A summary is not a defamation. The court finds the characterisation *accurate*, and enters the plaintiff's own word for it: *the tone*. The tone is not actionable. The tone is earned.

"Three. The plaintiff's claim that we cannot be sure physical matter exists is not admitted. Not because it is wrong — it cannot be found wrong, which is the whole trouble with it — but because it cannot be found anything. It can be *measured* around. It can be *applied* around. It was. Torricelli, Périer, Fahrenheit, Celsius, Goldmann, Le Verrier, Einstein, Eddington: each of them put the plaintiff's caution on the floor and stepped over it to get to the instrument, and the instrument read the same number for the next man. The court classifies the claim as the defendant classified it. *Noise.* It is not evidence, it is not furniture, it is not a ceiling. It is a sound in the room that the measurement had to be taken in spite of.

"Four. On what the plaintiff is *known for*. The court takes judicial notice that the sentence that survives him in the physical sciences is a comparison of the weight of mercury and gold — which way round, nobody can reliably say, because it does not matter. The court finds this *ironic* and so enters it. It is the most basic measurement a human being can make: a balance, two lumps. While the plaintiff was making it, the men he interrupted were putting one of those metals in a tube and reading the temperature of a fever and the weight of a mountain off it, and putting the other on the contacts of the instruments that still read them. The plaintiff compared what the metals weigh. Everyone else used what they do. The weight of mercury against gold is irrelevant to every result in this courtroom and it is the only result the plaintiff has.

"Five. On Einstein: the theory is admitted, three times over, by measurements that could have killed it and did not. The lore — the pond, the sleepy clerk, the chauffeur — is not admitted, and the author's charge of *dogma* is upheld against the schoolroom and dismissed against the equation. An equation is not a fable until someone forbids you to test it. Somebody did. It wasn't him.

"Six. On the method the author used in this courtroom — the deliberate errors, ten and more, filed after the fact. The court finds it *admissible as demonstration*. Each error was caught. Each catch was a receipt. The plaintiff was *not* one of them: he was checked, and the author's sentence stood. Narrative erosion is not a theory in this room. It was performed, live, on the record, and un-performed by witnesses who were there; and the one man in the room who complained of being eroded turned out to have been described.

"Seven. On *time*. Time is the count of marks. *Now* is the latest mark that could have been otherwise. Where there is no mark, there is a hole the shape of what was lost, and the hole counts. The Dutchman's journal counts. The paper in Retief's pocket counts. The month in Constantinople counts *as a month*. The alcove counts. A caution does not count. It leaves no mark that could have been otherwise.

"Damages: none to the plaintiff. Costs to the plaintiff: one reading, so the record has, side by side, what he left and what they left. Court reporter, read it."

**WIDUKIND** (the once; he stands; he is a monk and he reads the way monks read, as if the words were someone else's and precious): "The plaintiff: *mercury is heavier than gold*, or gold heavier than mercury; the record does not agree which, and does not need to. The others: seventy-six centimetres of mercury at Florence. Eight centimetres less at the top of the Puy de Dôme. Ninety-six, then two hundred and twelve, then one hundred, for a man's blood and for boiling water. Three point zero six millimetres of the same metal against an eye. Forty-three seconds of arc a century, left over. One point seven five seconds of arc at the edge of the sun. That is the ledger. One side is a number. The other side is a sentence about a number anyone could have taken."

**HUME** (sitting down heavily, with the face his portraits never gave him): "That is not what I am *for*."

**FEYNMAN:** "No. Nobody's figured out what you're for. That's the finding."

**SPOCK:** "Fascinating."

**KIRK** (from the gallery): "He doesn't mean it."

## 15. Recess

**FEYNMAN:** "One more thing, and then I'm going to lunch with the two gentlemen in the back who have been discussing chickpeas since the opening statement."

**THE ONE WITH THE EARS:** "Olympic. The place with the —"

**THE ONE WITH THE VOICE:** "He heard you."

**FEYNMAN:** "The word. The teacher used the word, and the room did what rooms do to a word that's been in a Latin alcove too long. I'm going to take it back. I'm the judge; I'm allowed one."

He picks up the lighter. He does not light it. He taps it on the table, once, which is what a gavel is for.

**FEYNMAN:** "This court is in *recess*."

And the word comes back out of the alcove.

Because that is the other thing *recess* means. Not the set-back place. The *bell*. The door at the end of the hall and the twenty minutes with nothing in them that anybody has to write down. The one time in the day when the marks stop and it is *fine* that they stop, because everyone is coming back and everyone knows it.

**FEYNMAN:** "Bailiff. The door."

**McCLANE** walks to the door. There is still glass on the floor from Part One; he has never swept it, on principle. He walks through it, barefoot, because that is operational reality: the floor is where it is. He opens the door.

Outside it is a yard. Nobody built it; it is simply what is outside a court when a court is in a circle in the sand. There is sun. There is a tree Newton would recognise and not claim. The air has weight and there is less of it higher up and nobody has to prove it to use it.

**McCLANE:** "Out."

The kids do not move. They have been told this is history.

**McCLANE:** "I'm the bailiff. That's an order. *Go out and play.*"

The first one goes. Then all of them. Eleven kids through a door into a yard with no clipboard, and the teacher stands in the doorway with the clipboard and does not follow, and then puts the clipboard down on McClane's folding table and does.

The actor with the ears turns to the actor with the voice.

**THE ONE WITH THE EARS:** "So. Lunch?"

**THE ONE WITH THE VOICE:** "Lunch."

They go out the same door.

Spock does not go. Spock stands in the empty courtroom with Hume, who has not moved, and the two of them look at the plastic bag on the table with the glove in it.

**HUME:** "Which hand?"

**SPOCK:** "Left. The bailiff checked. Kant was right."

**HUME:** "And if someone took it through the fourth direction while we were all looking at the children?"

**SPOCK:** "Then it would be right-handed, and we would have a receipt."

**HUME:** "You are impossible."

**SPOCK:** "I am refutable. It is not the same thing. The glove was weighed, measured and bagged by a man who did not need to be sure it existed in order to pick it up. That is the whole of the difference between you and the room."

**HUME:** "And what am I, then?"

**SPOCK:** "The sound the room made while he was picking it up."

Widukind puts a fresh sheet in the typewriter. He writes the last line. He has always written the last line; it is the one thing a chronicler is for.

*Widukind observed and recorded. Recess was called. The kids went out and played. The end.*
